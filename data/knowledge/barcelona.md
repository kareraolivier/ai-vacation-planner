# Barcelona Travel Notes

Barcelona is best on foot and by metro. The Gothic Quarter, El Born, and the beachfront can fill two days. Eixample is for Gaudi architecture and calmer cafes. Avoid unmarked "ticket sellers" at Sagrada Familia; book official timed entry.

## Local tips

Lunch is the value meal. Many kitchens close mid-afternoon. Pickpockets work Las Ramblas and the metro. Tapas culture is real, but neighborhood bars in Gracia or Poble Sec feel more local than the waterfront tourist strip.

## Hidden gems

Hospital de Sant Pau is less crowded than Recinte Modernista headlines suggest. Bunkers del Carmel has a city view at sunset. The beaches south of Barceloneta thin out if you keep walking.

## Weather-friendly ideas

The city is pleasant most of the year. On hot days, museums and the Palau de la Musica tour beat midday beach time. On rainy days, Casa Batllo, the maritime museum, and covered markets such as Santa Caterina are better than Park Guell queues.

## FAQs

Is one day enough? No. Give Barcelona at least three days if you want both architecture and the coast.

Should I visit Montserrat? Yes, on a clear day. Skip it in heavy fog or if you only have a short weekend.
