# Paris Travel Guide

Paris rewards walkers. The historic core is compact, the Metro is dense, and many signature sights sit within a 30-minute stroll of one another. First-time visitors usually combine one major museum, one neighborhood walk, and one evening viewpoint each day rather than trying to "see everything."

## Local tips

The Louvre is least crowded at opening and on Wednesday and Friday evenings. Reserve a timed ticket. If rain is likely, swap outdoor river walks for covered passages such as Galerie Vivienne, the Musee d'Orsay, or a long cafe lunch in Saint-Germain.

Bakeries open early. Buy a morning pastry and walk the Seine before tour buses arrive. Tap water is safe. Tipping is modest; service is included.

## Hidden gems

Skip the busiest Eiffel Tower slots and go to Rue de l'Universite or Trocadero at dusk. Promenade Plantee offers an elevated park walk. Canal Saint-Martin is better for evening picnics than the packed riverfront near Notre-Dame. The covered food halls at Marche des Enfants Rouges are excellent on a wet day.

## Weather-friendly ideas

On sunny days, prioritize Luxembourg Gardens, the Latin Quarter streets, and a Seine walk between Ile de la Cite and the bridges near the Louvre. On wet or cold days, favor Musee d'Orsay, Centre Pompidou, Shakespeare and Company browsing, and covered shopping galleries. Many cafes will let you linger if you order another drink.

## FAQs

How many days? Four to five days covers the highlights without rushing. A 3-day trip should pick either Versailles or a full Louvre morning, not both.

Is Paris expensive? Sit-down dinners and attractions add up on a luxury style. Markets, bakery lunches, and park picnics keep a budget trip reasonable.

What about neighborhoods? Stay in the Marais, Saint-Germain, or the 9th for walkability. Montmartre is beautiful but hillier and more spread out.
